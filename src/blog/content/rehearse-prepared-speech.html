Why rehearse with a timer

A speech that runs over has often never been timed in full. Reading your notes aloud feels shorter than a real delivery. A timer gives you the number, and the number tells you what to cut or where you have room.

This guide uses a 5 to 7 minute prepared speech. Change the numbers for your own project.

Step 1: plan the time

Split the speech into sections and give each one a budget that adds up to your target. Here is a plan for a speech that should finish at 6:00, with room to spare before the red card at 7:00.

SectionStartsLength
Opening0:001:00
Point 11:001:30
Point 22:301:30
Point 34:001:00
Closing5:001:00

Step 2: set up a rehearsal meeting

  1. On the home page create a meeting called something like "Rehearsal: My Ice Breaker". The date can be today.
  2. Press Add Speaker, enter your name and the role Prepared Speech, choose your project's preset and save.

You now have a roster with one line for the full speech.

Step 3: do a full run

  1. Press Start Timer on your row, put the timer where you can see it, and stand up.
  2. Press START and give the speech out loud, as you would on the day.
  3. Press STOP at the end. The time is saved and appears on the roster as the final time.

Look at the colour you ended on.

  • Plain screen: you finished before the minimum. You have room to add an example.
  • Green: within the window, and early. Good.
  • Yellow: on target.
  • Red: over. Cut something.

Step 4: rehearse by section

If a full run is over or under, find out which section is responsible. The timer has no built-in sections, but you can create one speaker per section and give each its own limits.

  1. Press Add Speaker again and use Custom Timing.
  2. Name the speaker after the section, for example "Opening", and use it as the role name too.
  3. Set the three times around the budget. For the opening, 0:45, 1:00 and 1:15 works well.
  4. Repeat for each section.

Time each section on its own, and cut or extend until each fits its budget. Then run the whole speech again.

Step 5: read the result

After a few runs, press Generate Report. The report shows your time against the limits and says whether it qualified. A speech that qualifies in rehearsal with a bit of room will usually survive nerves on the day.

Nerves change your pace. Many speakers speed up on the day, and some slow down. Aim to finish in the green or the yellow, not on the very edge of red.

Checklist for the day

  • You have done at least three full runs.
  • Your best run ended between the green and yellow cards.
  • You know where in the speech you will be at 5:00 and 6:00.
  • You have told the Timer your limits.
